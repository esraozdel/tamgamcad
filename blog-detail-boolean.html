Design Complex Parts Easily: What are Boolean Operations?

Author: Esra ÖZDEL | Date: November 6, 2026 | Category: CAD and Technical Drawing

When drawing in CAD programs, especially if you are preparing mechanical parts, machine gears, or CNC laser cutting templates, sometimes the desired shape is too complex to be drawn in one go using straight lines and arcs. In such cases, you can achieve the desired form by adding and subtracting basic geometric shapes (circles, squares, polygons) to each other almost like dough.

These shape merging and subtracting methods based on mathematical logic principles are called Boolean Operations. TamgamCAD adapts professional production to the mobile environment by bringing this powerful feature, which is usually found in heavy desktop software, to tablets and phones.

How Do Boolean Operations Work?

For Boolean operations to work, the shapes must intersect or touch each other, and they must be "closed geometries" (for example, a full circle or a closed polyline), not lines with open ends. Let's take a closer look at the 3 basic Boolean tools available in TamgamCAD:

1. Union

This operation fuses two or more overlapping closed shapes together to create a single, seamless, and continuous form.

Practical Example: Imagine creating a "T" or "L" profile by drawing end-to-end rectangles. Using the Union command, you can eliminate the inner intersecting lines between them and turn this profile into a single piece, solid surface.

2. Difference (Subtract)

It is perhaps the most frequently used Boolean tool. It is used to cut out and discard the area of a second geometry that overlaps onto a main (target) geometry.

Practical Example: You are going to drill screw holes on a steel plate. Draw a large rectangle, place circles representing the screw holes inside it. When you select the Difference tool and subtract the circles from the main plate, your production-ready CNC template with drilled holes is completed in seconds.

3. Intersection

This feature keeps only the common (intersecting) area of two overlapping shapes and deletes all remaining excesses. It is a method frequently used in engineering, especially to obtain organic or curved geometries.

Eliminate the Margin of Error in Production

Complex shapes can also be created with the traditional "Trim" tool; however, after the Trim operation, the lines are not integrated (closed) with each other. Lines left open-ended cause errors (Open Loop) in CNC machines or when Hatching is applied.

Because the results obtained with Boolean operations are always "closed" (Closed Polyline), they can be sent directly to laser cutting, 3D printers, or CAM software with zero errors.
